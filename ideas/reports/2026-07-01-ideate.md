# Ideate — 2026-07-01

**Focus (from invocation):** competitors + how the *general functionality* can be expanded and
made more useful. Explicitly **not** about adding more sources.

---

## What this is (updated read)

A hosted, multi-user **job-hunting command centre**: Go API + SolidStart frontend. It scrapes
startup boards on a 6h cron, scores every job **per-user** (heuristic *Relevance* gate → LLM
*Suitability* against an editable rubric), and lets users manage CV variants (Google-Doc tabs)
and an application pipeline with custom stages.

**Since the last ideation (2026-06-24), the frontier moved.** Two of the then-pending ideas
have effectively **shipped**:

- **Glass-box scoring** — `job_scores.reasoning` exists; `SuitabilityPanel` on job detail shows
  score + rationale + matched/missing skill chips; `scoring.tsx` exposes the editable rubric,
  relevance cutoff, and notify threshold; on-demand "Explain score".
- **AI settings / BYOK (Phase 1)** — `settings/ai.tsx`: per-user scoring + reasoning model
  pickers (default Haiku 4.5), Anthropic key input. (Multi-tenant *ingest* still single-user —
  `SCORING_USER_ID` — per `multi-tenant-byok.md` Phases 2–4.)

So "surface the reasoning" is done. The new question is: **turn the standing scored corpus into
higher-quality applications, and prove the score is honest.**

**Implicit thesis (unchanged, now more defensible):** *be selective and intelligent* — spend
scrape/LLM/attention only where it pays. The 2026 market has swung hard toward this.

## The competitive picture (this is the important part)

### The new doppelgänger: `career-ops` (OSS, ~56.7k★)

Verified via web search. [career-ops.org](https://career-ops.org/) does almost exactly your
thesis: rubric-guided LLM evaluation across **six dimensions, scored 1.0–5.0 with citations to
specific CV lines and JD requirements**, generates an **ATS-optimised PDF tailored per role**,
drafts answers to **Greenhouse/Ashby/Lever** questions, scans 150+ portals zero-token, and tracks
the pipeline in a **Go TUI**. It recommends *against* applying below 4.0.

**Why it doesn't kill you — and where you win.** It is **local, single-user, one-shot-per-JD,
run inside a coding CLI**. It has no persistent multi-user corpus, no standing market view, no
outcome memory, and no variant *library* (it generates one PDF per role). Everything it can't
structurally do maps to your architecture:

| career-ops (local one-shot) | This product (hosted standing corpus) |
|---|---|
| Scores a JD you hand it, then forgets | Holds a **scored corpus** (`jobs` + `job_scores`) that accumulates |
| No outcomes memory | Holds **applications + status history + response rate**, per user |
| Generates one tailored PDF | Holds **N CV variants** (`tracked_doc_tabs`) — selection, not generation |
| CLI, single applicant | Web app, **multi-user**, always-on cron |

### The incumbents (verified)

| Tool | Strength | Exploitable weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **Teal** | "Honest" Match Score, ATS keyword gap analysis | Keyword-oriented; builds resumes, doesn't hold your variants; score not validated against *your* outcomes |
| **Huntr** | Autofill (Workday/Greenhouse), CRM, AI resumes | Match score **overstates alignment** (false readiness); one resume; doesn't surface *which* keywords you're missing |
| **Simplify** | AI copilot autofill + pipeline | Autofill, not fit reasoning; no corpus-level intelligence |
| **career-ops** | Rubric+citations, tailored PDF, drafts answers | Local single-user one-shot; no standing corpus, no outcome calibration, no variant library |

### 2026 market backdrop (verified)

- Application volume exploded to **300+/role**; AI auto-apply flooded funnels → **backlash**.
- Employer **ghosting hit a 3-year high (53%)**; candidates now circulate ghosting-accountability lists.
- Platforms **suppress automation** — ~23% of auto-appliers restricted within 90 days.
- The one thing that measurably works: **per-role tailoring** lifts interview rate ~2% → 3–4%.

> **Eureka — the market converged on the wrong answer.** Everyone raced to *volume* (auto-apply,
> mass autofill) and it produced workslop, ghosting, and platform bans. First principles says the
> winning move is the opposite: **fewer, better, calibrated applications.** That is *already your
> thesis* — and your standing corpus + outcome data + variant library is the exact substrate for
> it. The incumbents can't pivot here without becoming a different product.

> **Eureka 2 — nobody validates their own score.** Every competitor shows a match/suitability
> score. **None** feed real outcomes back to check whether the score predicts responses. You hold
> `job_scores` **and** `applications` outcomes per user — you can ship the only *calibrated,
> honest* score in the category. It's read-only analytics over data you already store.

## Opportunity → solution ideas

Outcome wanted: **every application the user sends should be higher-fit and better-prepared than the
last, and the user should trust the score because it's been proven against their own results.**
Ideas span 6 surfaces (insights, jobs detail, cv-templates, applications, capture, overview).

Feasibility legend: 🟢 extends existing module · 🟡 new surface on existing arch · 🔴 significant bet.

### Adjacent (small extensions / finishing what's 60% there)

**1. Glass-box finish: inline reasoning + below-cutoff toggle** 🟢
- *Gap (Explore-confirmed):* reasoning shows only on the **detail page**, not as a jobs-list row
  expander; there's no toggle to reveal below-cutoff ("skipped") jobs in `jobs.tsx`. These are the
  unshipped tail of `glass-box-scoring.md` (Phases 2 & 4).
- *Build:* row expander reusing `SuitabilityPanel`; a "show skipped" filter chip on the list.
- *Uniquely positioned:* the data and component already exist — pure wiring.

**2. Paste-a-JD / bookmarklet capture** 🟢 *(your chosen capture MVP)*
- *Why:* Teal/Huntr/Simplify all win top-of-funnel with a capture surface; you have none outside
  the scraper. Rides the ingest+score path already in place.
- *Build:* a "paste a JD URL or raw text → score into my corpus" box + a one-click bookmarklet
  that POSTs the current page URL to the same ingest endpoint. No extension store, no per-site
  autofill upkeep.
- *Uniquely positioned:* ingest + per-user scoring already exist; this just adds a manual entry
  door to the same pipeline. Lets the corpus grow beyond what the cron finds.

**3. Follow-up nudges for stalled applications** 🟢
- *Why:* directly answers the 53%-ghosting reality. An application sitting N days in a stage with
  no movement gets a nudge to follow up.
- *Build:* reuse `applications` + `application_statuses` timestamps + the existing notify/digest
  infra. A daily check for stage-age > threshold.
- *Uniquely positioned:* notification + digest machinery already ships; this is a new query over
  existing timestamps.

### Expansion (new surface on existing architecture)

**4. Calibrated "Honest Score" — score-vs-outcome validation** 🟡 · *iteration on "Score-validated funnel analytics"* · **← recommended differentiation flagship**
- *Why:* **the single sharpest wedge vs every competitor** (Eureka 2). Huntr overstates, Teal is
  "more honest" but neither *proves* it. You hold score **and** outcome per user.
- *Build:* a new `insights.tsx` chart — bucket suitability scores, plot **actual response/interview
  rate per bucket** (a calibration curve), and auto-recommend the cutoff/notify threshold that best
  separates responders. Extends the existing `SourceQualityChart` pattern (already links source →
  outcome).
- *Uniquely positioned:* `job_scores` × `applications` status history, per user — data career-ops
  (one-shot) and the incumbents (no persistent outcome link) structurally lack.

**5. Best-CV-for-this-job + real gap analysis** 🟡 · *iteration on pending "Best-CV-for-this-job"* · **← recommended first build**
- *Why:* your variant library is the asset career-ops (one PDF) and Teal/Huntr (one resume) don't
  have. Today matched/missing chips are **JD-only** — not measured against *your* CV.
- *Build:* new `google.Client.FetchTabText` (verified missing — client only pulls tab titles + PDF
  today; same `drive.readonly` scope covers it) → LLM ranks the N variants against the JD → shows
  best variant + matched/missing **vs the chosen CV**. Reuses `SuitabilityPanel` UI, `tracked_doc_tabs`,
  BYOK LLM.
- *Uniquely positioned:* the **jobs × CV-variants × LLM** triangle — the documented moat, now
  finally exercised end-to-end.

**6. Tailored draft — cover letter + application answers** 🟡 · *iteration on pending "Tailored application draft"*
- *Why:* tailoring is the one thing proven to lift interview rate; career-ops drafts Greenhouse/
  Ashby/Lever answers. Match it, from JD × chosen CV.
- *Build:* once #5's CV-text fetch exists, one LLM call → cover letter + short-answer drafts,
  attached to the application record. Reuses BYOK model picker.
- *Uniquely positioned:* only product here that drafts from **your selected variant** against a
  **scored** job, not a generic profile.

**7. Company responsiveness & ghost-risk intelligence** 🟡
- *Why:* 53% ghosting is the market's rawest nerve. You alone hold cross-application company
  outcome memory.
- *Build:* aggregate `applications` by company → median time-to-response, response rate, "ghost
  risk" flag surfaced on `jobs_.$id` and as a jobs-list filter.
- *Uniquely positioned:* corpus-level company memory; a local one-shot tool can't accumulate it.

### Moonshot (significant bet, ties the corpus together)

**8. Selective Apply Copilot — hard-gated daily shortlist** 🔴 · *iteration on pending "Application Copilot"*
- *Why:* the "apply to fewer, better" product in one loop — the anti-spray flagship the market is
  begging for. career-ops says "don't apply below 4.0"; you can do that as a *standing daily
  ritual* over a real corpus.
- *Build:* daily, take corpus jobs ≥ threshold → for each, pre-pick best CV (#5) + draft (#6) +
  ghost-risk (#7) + a one-click "track application". A tight "here are today's 5 worth applying to,
  already prepped" surface on `overview.tsx`.
- *Uniquely positioned:* only possible because #4–#7 all sit on one standing multi-user corpus.
  This is the thing career-ops and the incumbents can't assemble.

## Recommended next build

**Start with #5 — Best-CV-for-this-job + real gap analysis.** It exercises the documented moat
end-to-end, it's the substrate #6 and #8 depend on, and it directly rides the only tactic proven
to work (tailoring). Its one unknown — fetching CV text — is exactly what a tracer bullet resolves.

**Tracer-bullet first slice (thin vertical, one job × one CV):**
1. Add `google.Client.FetchTabText(ctx, userID, docID, tabID) (string, error)` — one Docs API call
   for `documents/{docID}?fields=tabs(documentTab.body...)`, flattened to plaintext. No new scope
   (`drive.readonly` already granted). *This is the whole risk — prove it first.*
2. One handler: given a `job_id` + one `tracked_doc_tab`, fetch JD (`jobs.description`) + CV text,
   one LLM call (reuse the BYOK model + suitability prompt style) → `{matched[], missing[], fit_note}`.
3. On `jobs_.$id.tsx`, render it in a second `SuitabilityPanel`-style block: "vs *this* CV".
4. Only after that works: rank *all* variants and pick the best.

**Then:** #4 (Calibrated Honest Score) as the differentiation flagship — low lift, pure analytics
over data you already hold, and the one claim no competitor can make.

Next step for #5: run `write-a-prd` to spec the CV-gap handler and the `FetchTabText` contract.
