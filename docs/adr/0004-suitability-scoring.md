# ADR 0004 — Suitability from shared Jev answers and per-user picks

> Superseded in part by [ADR 0021](0021-gates-weighted-coverage-and-bands.md): the "any pick in a dimension matches" credit and the `k=3` prior in "Suitability is a pure function". Everything else here stands.

`jobs` is a shared catalog with no `user_id`. A Suitability score is one User's assessment of a Job, like an Application, so it lives in `job_scores (job_id, user_id)`, never on `jobs`. What the judge learns about a Job is shared; only a User's picks are theirs.

- **The judge.** Jev (`typesafe/jev-1.13` via OpenRouter's `/api/alpha/decisions`) answers typed questions against a `state` object of structured job fields and returns probabilities: no prose to parse, and job text is data rather than instructions. The endpoint is `alpha`, so its request/response structs stay private to scoring's `jev` package (`internal/scoring/internal/jev` under ADR 0011). It bills against each User's own `user_ai_credentials` (provider `"openrouter"`).
- **The answer bank.** ~160 fixed options (`scoring_options`, seeded by migration) in six dimensions (`tech`, `role`, `domain`, `seniority`, `work`, `stage`), each fixed in Go as `pair` (nice/avoid) or `multi` (nice only). Each option is one atomic Jev `choice` question (`yes`/`no`/`not_stated`), cached in `option_answers` keyed on `(job_id, fingerprint, question_hash, model)`.
- **Suitability is a pure function.** `suitability.compute` turns a User's picks and a job's cached answers into 0–100 plus one breakdown row per pick. Nice picks earn their dimension's weight once when any pick in it matches; avoid picks cost their weight only when the job has them; unknown answers count on neither side. A `k=3` prior pulls thin evidence toward 50.
- **Gated by interest, filtered before cost.** `SaveCanonical` queues one answer effect per job (`effect_outbox`, keyed `(job_id, fingerprint, model)`) only if some User is interested: a tracked Company or an enabled Source Target on that source (ADR 0007). Hard filters and the credential check run when the effect is processed, so Jev cost scales with relevant jobs, not board size.
- **Only picked questions are asked.** An effect asks the union of surviving Users' live picks (manual and text-extracted) minus what's cached, never the whole bank, so cost scales with picks rather than bank size. Picks on retired options are skipped. `jev.Client.Answer` batches questions to stay under Jev's 32k context.
- **Editing is cheap, and pays only for gaps.** Save runs `suitability.Recompute` over cached answers (free), then `FillMissingAnswers`: one effect (`first_discovery = false`, so no alert) per open scored job missing an answer to a picked question at its current fingerprint and model. It's idempotent; a sweep with no gaps queues nothing. The same sweep backs the "Refresh scores" button, which is how reopened jobs, failed effects and a `jev.Model` bump get repaired. Removing a pick, a blocklist or threshold change never calls Jev.

Trade-off: the weights and the 0.6 resolution threshold are Go constants with no per-user override, and picks can't express anything the bank doesn't cover. That's deliberate: atomic shared questions are what make answers cacheable and shared across Users. Asking only picked questions means a new pick shows `unknown` on older jobs until its backfill lands, and a reopened job stays `unknown` until the next Refresh. Jev also gives no rationale text, so a score has a breakdown but no written explanation.

- **`block` is a hard disqualifier, `avoid` is not.** `domain` picks may take a third stance, `block` (the `Stances` on the domain dimension in `scoring.Dimensions`), for cases like a company or industry blocklist. A `nice`/`avoid` pick only ever nudges the score; `block` is different in kind. Once Jev resolves a `block` pick's question `yes`, `compute` forces the row's `Effect` to `"blocked"` and the job's score to 0 regardless of every other pick, and the job is filtered out of that User's job list entirely (`jobsearch`'s `ListJobs`/`PageJobs` queries exclude any job whose cached breakdown contains a `blocked` row). A `block` pick that resolves `no` or `unknown` has no effect, same as an `avoid` pick the job lacks. `block` stays a separate stance rather than folding into `avoid`: `avoid` must stay a soft signal (one disliked technology in an otherwise good listing shouldn't auto-exclude it), so a hard-exclude use case needs a stance `avoid` can't safely be repurposed for.
